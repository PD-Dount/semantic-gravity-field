Physically inspired attention variants, in which tokens carry a mass and a capture radius and interact through a distance-decaying potential, promise content-adaptive sparsity that dense attention cannot offer. We implement one such proposal, a semantic gravity field, as a single parameterised attention layer and test it six ways. Algebraically the design is a softmax attention whose logit is a monotone warp of cosine similarity plus a per-token bias; at constant bandwidth the logit is exactly alpha*cos + beta*cos^2 + b_j (fit R2 = 1.000000), and the query-side mass is provably inert. Empirically: five arms are statistically indistinguishable in accuracy (0.206-0.214, chance 0.0625) while three cost 2.0-2.8x per optimiser step; the learned field strength shrinks under gradient descent from three different initialisations, so the optimiser votes against the mechanism; on a GPU the cost ratio grows with context (2.42x at 512 to 4.83x at 2048) while the effective number of attended keys stays within 0.4 percent of the causal baseline, so there is no sparsity to exploit; the iterated dynamical version is bistable, all-collapse or all-isolated, with no stable multi-cluster state up to N=8192; as a memory-eviction rule it is bit-identical to LRFU (2001); and fitted to the attention logits of a pretrained transformer the gravity basis is indistinguishable from parameter-matched controls (delta R2 <= 0.003) while the full field parameterisation fits worse. All tests are small-scale and use dense kernels, so we falsify deployment paths rather than the metaphor itself. The transferable output is a diagnostic protocol (control bases, gradient-sign voting, sparsity accounting, cost-ratio scaling) plus six implementation traps that silently invalidate otherwise correct experiments.
